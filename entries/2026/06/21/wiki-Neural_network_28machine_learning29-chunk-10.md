## Artificial Neural Networks — Resource Map and Domain Taxonomy

This page is the external links and navigation section of the Wikipedia article on Artificial Neural Networks. Rather than teaching concepts directly, it serves as a comprehensive index connecting ANNs to their parent domains (AI, complex systems, control theory, neuroscience) and cataloguing key architectures, people, and applications. It is most useful as an orientation map showing where ANNs fit in the broader ML landscape.

## Key Concepts

- **Neural network architectures** are categorized into distinct families: Transformer, RNN (LSTM, GRU, Echo State Network), CNN, MLP, Autoencoder (VAE), GAN, GNN, Mamba, Residual/Highway networks, and Neural Turing Machines / Differentiable Neural Computers
- ANNs sit at the intersection of four major fields: **Artificial Intelligence**, **Complex Systems**, **Control Theory**, and **Neuroscience**
- Within complex systems, ANNs relate to **evolution and adaptation** alongside genetic algorithms, evolutionary computation, and artificial life
- Within control theory, ANNs appear as an **advanced technique** alongside Kalman filters, fuzzy logic, and Lyapunov stability
- Core AI concepts that underpin ANNs include: backpropagation, gradient descent (SGD), attention, convolution, activation functions (softmax, sigmoid, rectifier), normalization (batchnorm), regularization, weight initialization, and loss functions
- The **bias-variance tradeoff**, **overfitting**, and **double descent** are fundamental training dynamics
- **Biologically inspired neural networks** with homeostatic regulation and adaptive repair mechanisms represent an active research direction (Hakim & Alam, 2025)

## Relationships

- **ANNs → Deep Learning → Machine Learning → AI**: ANNs are the foundational substrate for deep learning, which is a subset of ML, which is an application of AI
- **ANNs → Reinforcement Learning**: Connected through Q-learning, SARSA, policy gradient, and RLHF
- **ANNs → Generative AI**: Enabled by architectures like GANs, VAEs, diffusion models, and autoregressive transformers
- **ANNs → Control Theory**: Used for neural control, model predictive control, and as an advanced technique in control systems
- **ANNs → Neuroscience**: Artificial neural networks are modeled after biological neural networks; computational neuroscience and neuromorphic engineering bridge the two
- **ANNs → Self-Driving Cars**: Listed as a core enabling technology alongside LIDAR, radar, and computer vision
- **ANNs → Complex Systems**: Positioned within evolution/adaptation, connecting to swarm intelligence, genetic algorithms, and self-organization

## Exam-Relevant Points

- **Know the architecture families and their abbreviations**: Transformer, RNN, LSTM, GRU, CNN, MLP, VAE, GAN, GNN, ViT (Vision Transformer)
- **Key historical figures to associate with neural networks**: McCulloch & Pitts (first mathematical model), Rosenblatt (perceptron), Hopfield (Hopfield networks), Hinton/LeCun/Bengio (deep learning pioneers), Schmidhuber (LSTM), Vaswani (Transformer), Fukushima (neocognitron/CNN precursor), Werbos (backpropagation), Linnainmaa (automatic differentiation)
- **Activation functions**: softmax, sigmoid, rectifier (ReLU) — know what each does and where each is used
- **Training concepts**: backpropagation, SGD, batch normalization, regularization, weight initialization, bias-variance tradeoff, double descent
- **ANNs bridge multiple disciplines** — expect questions placing them in context of control theory, neuroscience, or complex systems, not just AI
- **Modern extensions**: attention mechanism, Transformer architecture, diffusion models, RLHF, RAG, prompt engineering — these represent the current frontier built on ANN foundations
