## Machine Learning — Key References and Domain Taxonomy

This page comprises the bibliography, further reading, external links, and navigational taxonomy from the Wikipedia article on Machine Learning. It maps ML's position within the broader fields of Artificial Intelligence and Computer Science, lists foundational and contemporary textbooks, and catalogs the major sub-topics, applications, architectures, and notable contributors.

## Key Concepts

- **ML within AI taxonomy**: Machine learning is classified under Artificial Intelligence, alongside automated reasoning, reinforcement learning, diffusion models, autoregressive models, prompt engineering, and neuro-symbolic AI.
- **ML within CS taxonomy** (ACM 2012 Classification): ML falls under Artificial Intelligence, with sub-categories of Supervised, Unsupervised, Reinforcement, Multi-task, and Cross-validation.
- **Core AI concepts linked to ML**: Loss functions, regression, bias-variance tradeoff, overfitting, gradient descent (SGD), backpropagation, attention, convolution, normalization, activation functions (softmax, sigmoid, rectifier), regularization, data augmentation.
- **Neural network architectures**: Transformer, Vision Transformer (ViT), RNN, LSTM, GRU, Echo State Network, MLP, CNN, Residual Networks, Highway Networks, Mamba, Autoencoder, VAE, GAN, GNN, Neural Turing Machine, Differentiable Neural Computer.
- **Key application areas**: Computer vision, speech recognition, NLP, LLMs, generative AI, self-driving cars, game playing (AlphaGo/AlphaZero), robotics, healthcare, text-to-image/video generation.

## Relationships

- **ML to AI**: ML is a core sub-discipline of AI, connected to knowledge representation, planning, search, NLP, and computer vision.
- **ML to Statistics/Mathematics**: Draws on probability, statistics, information theory, numerical analysis, discrete mathematics, and optimization.
- **ML to Software Engineering**: Relies on programming languages, software frameworks, and development processes.
- **ML to Hardware**: Depends on GPU acceleration, system-on-chip designs, and energy-efficient computing.
- **ML to Security**: Intersects via adversarial ML, cryptography, and intrusion detection.
- **ML to Applications**: Feeds into generative AI, autonomous systems, healthcare, education, and scientific computing (computational biology, physics, chemistry).

## Exam-Relevant Points

- **Foundational textbooks to know by name**: Russell & Norvig (*AIMA*, 4th ed. 2020), Bishop (*Pattern Recognition and ML*, 2006), Hastie/Tibshirani/Friedman (*Elements of Statistical Learning*), Murphy (*Probabilistic ML*, 2022), Alpaydin (*Introduction to ML*, 4th ed. 2020).
- **Historical reference**: Solomonoff's 1956 "An Inductive Inference Machine" from the Dartmouth Summer Research Conference — one of the earliest ML-related works.
- **ML sub-categories per ACM**: Supervised, Unsupervised, Reinforcement, Multi-task, Cross-validation.
- **Key architectural families**: Transformers (including ViT), RNNs (LSTM, GRU), CNNs (including ResNets), Autoencoders (VAE), GANs, GNNs, Mamba (state-space models).
- **Notable figures in ML/AI**: Turing, McCulloch, Pitts, Minsky, McCarthy, Rosenblatt, Werbos, Hinton, LeCun, Bengio, Schmidhuber, Ng, Goodfellow, Hassabis, Vaswani, Sutskever, Chollet.
- **Double descent** is listed as a core AI concept — know it alongside the classical bias-variance tradeoff.
- **RLHF** (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback) and **self-supervised learning** are categorized as core AI concepts, not just LLM techniques.
